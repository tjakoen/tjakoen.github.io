# Have the AI draw it before it builds it

Status: DRAFT script for Tjakoen to read aloud and edit. Not yet read or checked by him.

Same format as the Harbor script: one idea a beat, about 20 to 45 seconds, names the phrase in the note that lights up, and adds the story, the why or the practice instead of repeating the page. This is a journey and an invitation, not a tutorial. Define a term in a clause and tell the listener to ask their agent. Claude Academy already covered the tools.

## Stop 1: The sentence that costs the most

**Beat 1**
highlight: intro | That's not what I meant.

You've said this sentence. After the AI builds something fast and sure of itself, exactly to what you described. The problem was the description. When the AI types, the scarce thing is intent, and one sentence carries very little of it.

**Beat 2**
highlight: why-drawing-matters-more-now | A drawing is the walk you skipped.

When you type code yourself, you learn the shape of it by walking through it. When the AI types it, you skip the walk. A drawing gives it back to you, in a few minutes.

**Beat 3**
highlight: why-drawing-matters-more-now | You don't have to draw it.

And here's the good news. You don't have to draw it. The AI is very good at drawing. What matters isn't who held the pen. It's that you and the AI end up with the same picture, written down.

**Beat 4**
highlight: why-drawing-matters-more-now | the AI generates whatever the code can produce

On my own projects I keep a design folder, and the AI generates whatever the code can produce, like the ERD from the database schema. That's heavier than you need. The point is these are old, useful drawings, and you only have to know they exist so you can ask for the right one.

**Digest:** The AI builds what you described, and what you described had holes. A drawing is the walk you skipped, and the AI can draw it for you. What matters is a shared, written-down picture of the plan.

**Trial:** Think of the last time you said that is not what I meant. Write down what you had not decided yet.

**Runes:**
the-walk-you-skipped | A drawing is the walk you skipped.
shared-picture | A shared, documented understanding is the goal

## Stop 2: Six drawings, one question each

**Beat 1**
highlight: six-drawings-one-question-each | Stick figures on the left, ovals on the right

Use case diagram. Who uses it, and what can they do. Draw it before anything else, because it's where you find out you have two kinds of user, not one.

**Beat 2**
highlight: six-drawings-one-question-each | where does information go?

Data flow. Where does information travel. It's the drawing that shows data moving somewhere you didn't plan. A static portfolio can skip it.

**Beat 3**
highlight: six-drawings-one-question-each | what does it remember?

The ERD. What does the thing remember, and how are those things related. For your portfolio, an entry has a date, a title, what you learned, and a link.

**Beat 4**
highlight: six-drawings-one-question-each | Gray boxes.

Wireframe is gray boxes, on purpose. What goes on each screen, with no one arguing about colors. The mockup is the same thing in real colors and type.

**Beat 5**
highlight: six-drawings-one-question-each | the thousandth screen looks like the first

The design system is the reusable parts. I built one, GRAIN, and I'll tell you about it later. The idea is that the thousandth screen should look like the first.

**Beat 6**
highlight: six-drawings-one-question-each | Reach for the one that answers the question

You won't need all six. Pick the one that answers the question you can't answer yet, and ask the AI to draw it. Every one has a good tutorial, linked in the note. I'm not teaching them. I'm telling you which one to ask for.

**Digest:** Use case, who does what. Data flow, where information goes. ERD, what it remembers. | Wireframe, what is on each screen. Mockup, how it looks. Design system, the reusable parts. | You will not need all six. Reach for the one that answers your open question.

**Trial:** Look at your portfolio brief and name which one of the six answers the question you cannot answer yet. For a portfolio, a use case diagram and a wireframe are plenty.

**Runes:**
one-question-each | Reach for the one that answers the question

## Stop 3: Let the AI hold the pen

**Beat 1**
highlight: let-the-ai-hold-the-pen | Draw a use case diagram for this.

Take the brief from the last lesson, paste it in, and say this. Ask for it as Mermaid, a plain-text way of describing diagrams. Text matters, because you can save it, compare it and change it.

**Beat 2**
highlight: let-the-ai-hold-the-pen | Then read it, and correct it. That's the job.

This is your part. The AI draws, you decide. Say, no, a visitor can't add entries, only I can. Each correction costs nothing now. And if you can't tell whether a box is right, ask it to explain that box.

**Beat 3**
highlight: let-the-ai-hold-the-pen | A common, written-down understanding

When you both agree, save it in the project, next to your brief. The AI forgets between sessions, so a drawing in the chat is gone tomorrow. A common, written-down understanding is what stops that's not what I meant.

**Beat 4**
highlight: make-the-teammate-ask-questions | Ask me five questions before you build anything.

And the trick from the first lesson. Hand it the drawing and say this. It turns a teammate who guesses into one who checks.

**Beat 5**
highlight: your-turn | wrong by the third change

One honest limit, and here I might be wrong about how much you need. A drawing is wrong by the third change if nobody updates it, and a wrong diagram is worse than none. For something tiny, skip all of this. Make them small, and ask the AI to update them.

**Digest:** Describe it and let the AI draw it, ideally in text it can update. Read it and correct it. That is your part. | Save the agreed drawing in the project, so every session works from the same plan. | A drawing is only worth keeping while it matches the code.

**Trial:** Give your AI your portfolio brief and ask for a use case diagram as Mermaid, then a gray-box wireframe of the roadmap page. Read them, fix one thing, ask what is missing, then save both next to the brief. Or draw it by hand, or draw something stranger.

**Runes:**
save-it | A common, written-down understanding
make-it-ask | Ask me five questions before you build anything.
