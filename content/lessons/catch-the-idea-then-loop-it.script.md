# Catch the idea, then loop it

Status: DRAFT script for Tjakoen to read aloud and edit. Not yet read or checked by him.

Same format as the Harbor script: one idea a beat, about 20 to 45 seconds, names the phrase in the note that lights up, and adds the story, the why or the practice instead of repeating the page. This is a journey and an invitation, not a tutorial. Catching an idea in one line, turning one into a brief before you build, and keeping the inbox honest.

## Stop 1: Catch it in one line

**Beat 1**
highlight: intro | Most of them vanish.

Once an AI does the typing, ideas arrive faster than you can build them. A thing that took a month takes an evening, so every annoyance becomes a project. Most of them vanish. You tell yourself you will remember, and then it is Thursday.

**Beat 2**
highlight: catch-it-in-one-line | Keep the inbox ugly and the lines short.

Keep the inbox somewhere you can reach from anywhere. A notes app on your phone, or a plain text file that syncs to it. Use whichever you will actually open, and keep it ugly and the lines short.

**Beat 3**
highlight: catch-it-in-one-line | A catch that takes longer than ten seconds won't get done on the bus.

The fix is one inbox you can reach from anywhere, and one line per idea. What it is, and the problem it solves if you know. Do not design it. A catch that takes longer than ten seconds will not get done on the bus.

**Digest:** Cheap building means more ideas than evenings, and most vanish. | Keep one inbox you can reach from anywhere, and write one line per idea. | Do not design it yet. A catch longer than ten seconds will not get done.

**Trial:** For one day, write every idea down in one line, wherever you catch it, without judging any of them.

**Runes:**
intro | Most of them vanish.
catch-it-in-one-line | A catch that takes longer than ten seconds won't get done on the bus.

## Stop 2: A catch is not a brief

**Beat 1**
highlight: then-dont-build-from-the-line | The line in your inbox is a catch, not a brief.

Here is the mistake a cheap build tempts you into. The line in your inbox is a catch, not a brief. Paste it straight into an agent and you get something confident and a little beside the point. You get the teammate from the first note.

**Beat 2**
highlight: then-dont-build-from-the-line | ask it to interview you, one question at a time

So there is a step in between, and it is the one people skip. Pick one idea and turn it into the three-part brief. Ask your AI to interview you, one question at a time, until the brief is complete. Then you edit it, because that is where your judgment goes in.

**Beat 3**
highlight: then-dont-build-from-the-line | figure: From an idea to a built thing

From there you know the path. Draw what needs drawing, write a plan with a done line per step, and build one step at a time. The inbox feeds the brief, the brief feeds the plan, and the plan feeds the loop.

**Digest:** The line in your inbox is a catch, not a brief. | Pick one idea and ask your AI to interview you, one question at a time, until the brief is complete. | The inbox feeds the brief, the brief feeds the plan, and the plan feeds the loop.

**Trial:** Pick the idea that surprised you most and ask your AI to interview you into a three-part brief. Change one line and save it next to your plan.

**Runes:**
then-dont-build-from-the-line | The line in your inbox is a catch, not a brief.
then-dont-build-from-the-line | ask it to interview you, one question at a time

## Stop 3: Most ideas stay in the inbox

**Beat 1**
highlight: most-ideas-should-stay-in-the-inbox | most of your ideas should never get built

An inbox that only grows is a guilt pile, so here is the hard part. Most of your ideas should never get built. Look at the list a week later and a lot of them already look smaller. That is the inbox working. Let them die cheaply.

**Beat 2**
highlight: most-ideas-should-stay-in-the-inbox | one active project at a time

One rule worth trying: one active project at a time, with one next step you could start in ten minutes. Everything else waits. When you are tempted by a second thing, add a line instead.

**Beat 3**
highlight: your-turn | a beautiful inbox and nothing shipped

And where I might be wrong. A tidy capture habit can turn into procrastination, a beautiful inbox and nothing shipped. And some ideas are better chased the minute they show up. Find the number of projects that does not leave you feeling behind.

**Digest:** An inbox that only grows is a guilt pile. | Most of your ideas should never get built, so let them die cheaply. | Try one active project at a time. When you are tempted by a second, add a line instead.

**Trial:** Keep the inbox going for a week, then delete the ideas that already look smaller, and tell me which one you were glad to delete.

**Runes:**
most-ideas-should-stay-in-the-inbox | most of your ideas should never get built
most-ideas-should-stay-in-the-inbox | one active project at a time
