# The AI says it is done. Make it prove it.

Status: DRAFT script for Tjakoen to read aloud and edit. Not yet read or checked by him.

Same format as the Harbor script: one idea a beat, about 20 to 45 seconds, names the phrase in the note that lights up, and adds the story, the why or the practice instead of repeating the page. This is a journey and an invitation, not a tutorial. Define a term in a clause and tell the listener to ask their agent. Claude Academy already covered the tools.

## Stop 1: Done is a claim

**Beat 1**
highlight: intro | Done! Everything is working and all the tests pass.

You've heard this. It feels great for ninety seconds, and then the button does nothing. The AI isn't lying. It's telling you what it believes, like a teammate who says yeah I looked at it without opening the file.

**Beat 2**
highlight: intro | Most of my prompting isn

Most of my prompting isn't build this. It's check this. A model is fluent enough to sound right exactly when it's wrong. And I've been fooled by my own checks, too. I once trusted one for three weeks before counting, and it had run once in a hundred and sixty-five sessions.

**Beat 3**
highlight: done-is-a-claim-not-a-result | They're what you check against.

This is why the brief, the drawing and the plan matter. They're what you check against. Without them, is it done has no answer. With them it has three.

**Beat 4**
highlight: make-it-show-you-the-evidence | show me the evidence that it's true

Don't ask is it done. Ask for evidence for every done line. Something you can see. Command output, a screenshot, a test that failed before and passes now.

**Digest:** Done is a claim, not a result. | Check it against the brief, the drawing and the plan. | Ask for evidence you can see for each done line.

**Trial:** Take the last step your AI called finished on your portfolio and ask it to show evidence for each done line.

**Runes:**
done-is-a-claim-not-a-result | They're what you check against.

## Stop 2: Run it, then ask fresh eyes

**Beat 1**
highlight: run-it-yourself | Open the thing and use it the way a user would

Then do the part nobody can do for you. Open it and use it like a user. A minute or two. And skim the list of changed files against your leave-alone list. Reading a diff gets easier fast, and it's the skill I'd most encourage you to build.

**Beat 2**
highlight: then-ask-a-fresh-pair-of-eyes | It's the same reason you don't mark your own exam.

Now the trick. Open a brand new session, give it the brief, the plan and the changes, and ask it to find what's wrong. The session that built something already believes it. It's the same reason you don't mark your own exam.

**Beat 3**
highlight: then-ask-a-fresh-pair-of-eyes | my experience and not a measurement

And be honest about that. It's my experience and not a measurement. Test it yourself on something small. If it finds nothing, you've earned a little trust in that piece of work.

**Beat 4**
highlight: then-ask-a-fresh-pair-of-eyes | figure: from done to proven

Put the whole chain in one picture. The AI says it's done, which is only a claim. You ask for the evidence on each done line, and you run it yourself the way a user would. The shaded step is the fresh session, the one that didn't write the code. Last, whatever it keeps missing goes in the brief file, so the next session reads it.

**Digest:** Use it yourself like a user, and read the list of changed files. | Give a fresh session the brief, plan and changes and ask it to find problems. | That is my experience, not a measurement. Test it on something small.

**Trial:** Run your last piece of work yourself, then open a new session and ask it to review the brief, plan and changes.

**Runes:**
then-ask-a-fresh-pair-of-eyes | It's the same reason you don't mark your own exam.

## Stop 3: Write down what it keeps missing

**Beat 1**
highlight: write-down-what-it-keeps-missing | I write it down in the file where the brief lives

When I catch the same miss twice, I don't just fix it. I write it down in the file where the brief lives. Next session reads it. That list is your checklist, and it's the start of making this your own.

**Beat 2**
highlight: write-down-what-it-keeps-missing | worth automating

My loop runs checks like these automatically, and PANTRY has a doctor that checks the loop is wired up. But I did it by hand first, because that's how I found out which checks were worth automating. The best check of all runs itself, and that's the next lesson.

**Beat 3**
highlight: your-turn | You're still the person accountable for what ships

And here's where I might be wrong. None of this makes mistakes impossible. A fresh reviewer can miss things or invent problems. You're still the person accountable for what ships, so you still have to understand it.

**Beat 4**
highlight: your-turn | Write down anything the review found that the first session didn't.

Your turn is the whole thing. Evidence, run it yourself, fresh session, write down what the review found. Then add your first roadmap entry, in your own words. The AI can fix your typos, but the words are yours.

**Digest:** When you catch the same miss twice, write it in the file your sessions read. | Do the checks by hand first, so you learn which ones are worth automating. | You are still accountable for what ships.

**Trial:** Ask for evidence, run it yourself, review it in a fresh session, and write down anything the review found that the first session missed. Then add your first roadmap entry, in your own words.

**Runes:**
your-turn | You're still the person accountable for what ships
