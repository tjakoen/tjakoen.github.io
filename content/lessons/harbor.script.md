## Stop 1: Before you set off

Hi. I'm Tjakoen, and before we go anywhere I want to tell you why this exists at all.

I think getting good at AI matters. Not a little. A lot. And not in the sense of knowing which button to press, but in the sense of knowing how to work with it, how to point it at a problem, and how to tell when it has quietly done something wrong. That is a skill. It can be learned, and it can be learned badly.

Here is the uncomfortable part. Where I teach, they do not really teach this. As far as I have seen, the subject sits somewhere between the courses, and nobody quite owns it. So people pick it up on their own, from whatever they happen to stumble into, and a lot of them pick up the habits before they pick up the judgment. I would rather you got the judgment first.

So let me give you the one idea I keep coming back to, because the rest of this path hangs on it. I care about builders more than coders.

When I think about what I actually enjoyed in programming, it was never the typing. It was building. Taking something that did not exist, deciding what it should be and how it should be shaped, and then watching it stand up. The code was how I got there. It was not the point.

So I want you to see yourself as a builder, not a coder. Think of the difference between an architect and a construction worker. Both matter. But the architect decides what the building is, where the load goes, and what would make it fall down. That is my view of where the value sits now, and it is the part a machine cannot do for you. You can hand the bricklaying to the AI. You cannot hand it the drawing.

I will be honest about what I am not promising. I am not promising this makes you employable, or fast, or finished. It is a way of looking at the work, and the rest is practice.

Now, what this actually is. This is a guided path through my own notes on working with AI. Fourteen quests, spread across the stages after this one. They run in order of difficulty. The intro notes come first, and the deep technical ones wait for the end, so by the time you reach them you have the ground to stand on.

You are standing at the Harbor right now. It is its own little island, and it is the only place on the map that points outside the site. To move on, press Continue, and you will cross to the mainland. Each quest is cut into stops, and each stop is one short recording like this one. As you listen, runes light up in the margin of the note when an idea gets taught, and they collect so you can find them again. At the end of every stop there is a trial, one small thing you can do in about five minutes. Do it. That is where the idea actually lands.

Your progress saves as a file or a code you keep. And I want to be clear about it: progress here is self-reported. Nothing on this site can check that you did the work. It only knows what you tell it. So the only person it can fool is you.

One more honest thing. This is a first draft. It is what an online course here could look like, and I am building it in the open. Some of it will be rough, and some of it will change.

Before you start, one stop outside the site. This path assumes you have already had a first conversation with Claude and know the basics. If you have not, go to Claude Academy first. The address is academy dot claude dot com. Get the basics there, then come back.

When you are ready, the first stop is Ten Times Zero. It is the belief underneath everything else here, and it is a good place to start.
