Infosession: Navigating Tech, the general assembly for the 2026-27 year, on Google Meet, seven in the
evening on a Saturday. About 60 students chose that over their own weekend, which raises the bar on
what you owe them. Three of us took the speaker list and came at the same question from different
sides. Louella Salenga, a professor at the university, took the ethics of AI in learning and teaching.

## What I argued, and where I got it from

I did not bring a theory. I brought what my own year looks like: what I hand to a model, what I refuse
to hand over, and the times I moved fast in the wrong direction because I did not read what came back.
The whole argument fits in one line I keep coming back to. AI is a multiplier, not an addend, so ten
times zero is still zero. The tool scales whatever you already are. If that is a lot, it is a lot more.
If it is nothing, you get a very fast nothing, and you get it in a language you cannot debug.

So the talk was mostly about the unglamorous half. Read what it hands you. Prove it before you ship it.
Keep getting better at the thing you are delegating, because the day you cannot check the work is the
day you stop being the one doing it.

## The question that made the night

In the Q and A a student asked the one I came for: with vibe coding, what should a student actually do
to skill up? My answer was deliberately boring, because it has not changed since before any of this.
Ace your classes. Teach yourself things nobody assigned you. Use the model to learn faster, not to hand
in something you cannot read.

Then the rule I would put on the wall if they let me: **don't vibe anything you can't code yourself.**
Not because using AI is cheating, but because the moment the generated thing breaks, the only person
who can save you is the version of you that understood it.

## Personal projects versus payroll

Vibe code your own projects. Weekend tools, side apps, the thing you are building because you want it
to exist. That is exactly the right place for it, and I do it too.

Production is a different job. Enterprise software carries compliance, security and reliability, and
none of those are answered by vibes. They are answered by someone who can read the diff, name the
failure mode, and be accountable for it at two in the morning. That is why we hire coders, not vibers.
The market is not paying for prompts. It is paying for judgment that survives contact with a real
system.

## The honest limit

This is a talk, not evidence. I can tell you what I have watched teams do well and badly, and I can
show my own receipts, but I have not run a study on students and I am not going to pretend otherwise.
The deck is the same one I gave: [ten times zero](/talks/ten-times-zero/#1), and the long-form version
lives in [the note](note:ten-times-zero). The images here are still the promo graphics, not the night
itself, until a screenshot from the call reaches me.
