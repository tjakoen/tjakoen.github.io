## 130 names, and I grade people like them for a living

That is the count off the booth sheet. It does not include the students who handed over a resume and
walked on, or the ones who scanned the QR code for the job postings and thought about it later. So
the honest version is that 130 is the floor, and I have no idea what the ceiling was.

Here is what made the day strange. I teach software engineering. The students in my classes are a
year or two behind the ones who were queueing at that table, asking the same questions with the same
nerves. In a classroom I am the person telling them what to learn. At that booth I was the person on
the other side deciding what the learning was worth.

Nobody warns you how much those two jobs argue with each other.

## What the booth actually is

Five of us behind one table: Christelle Salceda, Jun Bolotaolo, Beili Cepe, Jake Regunay, and me.
[Career Team](https://careerteam.com) builds workforce software, which is a dry way of saying the
systems that sit between a person who needs work and a program that exists to help them find it. The
company has been at it since 1996, mostly for the United States, and the platform is called Career
Edge.

The students came from across the region, not only from the host campus. Most of them wanted the same
two things: whether we take interns, and what we expect somebody to already know. Both answers are in
the talk I gave later the same day, which has [its own post](/calendar/yses-uplb-company-talks).

## The part I keep thinking about

A booth is a very efficient machine for collecting optimism. You stand there for a day, people give
you their names, and everyone leaves feeling like something happened.

Something did, but let me be accurate about what. 130 sign-ups is 130 people who were interested
enough to write it down. It is not 130 interviews and it is certainly not 130 jobs. Any recruiting
number quoted without that caveat is doing a job the number cannot do.

What I can say plainly is that the queue was long, the questions were good, and almost none of them
were about salary. They were about whether the work is real.

EXPE21ENCE is put on by [YSES](https://yses.org), the Young Software Engineers'
Society at UP Los Baños, and this was their twenty-first year of it. Job fair, hackfest, and company
talks in one run. The officers handled the whole thing, right down to the certificate in the photo,
and they did it while being students themselves.
