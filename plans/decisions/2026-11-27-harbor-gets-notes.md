# Decision: the Harbor gets its own notes

- **Date:** 2026-11-27
- **Amends:** the line in plans/quest-map.md that says "No Harbor notes. The first stop points out to
  Claude Academy for the basics."
- **Plan:** [plans/harbor-curriculum.md](../harbor-curriculum.md)
- **Status:** open. The direction below is decided. The questions at the end are not.

## What was decided

These come from the owner, in conversation, and are recorded as stated.

1. **Audience.** Students and juniors, roughly third or fourth year, who use a chat assistant and have
   never built their own AI loop, standards or harness. Programming, front end, back end, React,
   JavaScript and Next are assumed, and are not taught here.
2. **Purpose.** Inspire and enable. Leave the reader feeling able to build, not afraid. Not a to-do list.
3. **Stance.** No claim to be an expert or an authority. "This is how I work and it works for me."
   Readers are told to try things, build their own version and share it back.
4. **Credentials.** Only the real ones: daily use, a stack built with AI, a team that copied the idea
   and made it their own, and a long teaching practice. Badges are for fun, not authority.
5. **The Harbor starts with Claude Academy.** The free lessons are the first outside step. Once a reader
   has the basics, the loop works with any harness, from Claude Code to Codex to DeepSeek. If a setup is
   not working, the reader works with the AI to make it work.
6. **Teaching method.** Plan before you build, and know what you expect to see at the end, because the
   agent cannot think for you. The agent is a junior on the team who needs direction.
7. **Fundamentals are pointed at, not taught.** Software engineering concepts (framing, mockups, data flow
   diagrams, a user-centred drawing, clean code) are covered briefly, for why they matter more with AI.
8. **Individuality over standardising.** Everyone's relationship with agentic coding is personal. Copy,
   paste and adapt, then learn by building something that fits you. Team standards come later and are
   a separate job.
9. **Themes to cover somewhere in the path:** capturing ideas fast; planning before building; keeping
   momentum when usage runs out; tracking many ideas at once; making output sound like you; the job
   value of being able to talk about your AI work with depth.
10. **Scope of this step.** Brainstorm the notes for the first island first. Write nothing for
    publication until the list is agreed.

## What is proposed, not decided

- The eight Harbor notes in the plan, their order and their titles.
- Whether the "junior on a team" metaphor and the Harbor script's "architect, not construction worker"
  metaphor are both kept. A suggestion: the architect is what you decide, the junior is how you supervise.

2. One track. Readers who lead teams skip ahead.
3. The person drawing is a use case diagram.
4. No curriculum is needed. The basics are reintroduced in short and linked out to outside sources.
5. About six months of daily use is the number.

Also decided: the drawing note is a map of the artifacts (ERD, DFD, use case, wireframe, mockup, design
system) that links out. The browser story stays in The Browser Grew Up and is linked, not repeated.
