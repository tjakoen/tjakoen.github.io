📚 Reading · the people I learn from

Most of what I build started as something I read.

These are the people whose work I keep going back to. Each entry says what I took from them and links to the place in my own writing where it shows up, so you can see the idea in use rather than take my word that it mattered. Go read them first. They said it better.

Brad Frost, 2013 · Design

Atomic Design

A method for building interfaces as design systems: small atoms combine into molecules, molecules into organisms, and those into templates and pages.

Why it is here. It is more or less my love language for frontend work, and it is the shape GRAIN is built in.

Read it free online

Addy Osmani · AI

Beyond Vibe Coding

A practical book on building software with AI assistants, past the point where prompting alone carries you.

Why it is here. The seventy percent problem, and why the last stretch is where AI work rots. Every gate in my setup exists because of that last stretch.

Open the book

Addy Osmani · AI

Loop Engineering

An essay arguing that durable AI work is built from a few reusable parts in a loop, rather than from one clever prompt.

Why it is here. It is an essay, not a book, so it stands thin on the shelf. It is the framing One loop, every repo applies.

Read the essay

Josh Kaufman, 2010 · Business

The Personal MBA

A self-taught business education: the core ideas of value creation, marketing, sales, finance and people, each in a short chapter.

Why it is here. I learned business by starting five of them, and not one worked out the way I planned, which taught me more than success would have. This is the book I wish I had read before the first one.

Visit the book’s site

Daniel Kahneman, 2011 · Mind

Thinking, Fast and Slow

A Nobel-winning psychologist on the two systems of thought: the fast, intuitive one and the slow, deliberate one, and the biases that live between them.

Why it is here. My first answer is usually the fast one, and the fast one is usually the one I should check.

Robert Greene, 1998 · Business

The 48 Laws of Power

Forty-eight rules of power, each drawn from historical figures who used it well or lost it badly.

Why it is here. I read it to recognise the moves, not to make them. Knowing how office politics work made it easier to stay out of them.

Ryan Holiday and Stephen Hanselman, 2016 · Philosophy

The Daily Stoic

A year of short daily readings from the Stoic philosophers, one page and one reflection for each day.

Why it is here. One page a morning, and most days it comes back to the same thing: what is mine to control, and what is not.

Visit Daily Stoic

Edith Hall, 2018 · Philosophy

Aristotle's Way

A classicist on how Aristotle's ethics apply to an ordinary modern life: decisions, friendship, work and happiness.

Why it is here. A good life is something you practise, the same way you get good at a craft.

Donald Robertson, 2019 · Philosophy

How to Think Like a Roman Emperor

A cognitive psychotherapist on Marcus Aurelius, using his life to teach Stoic techniques for handling stress, anger and fear.

Why it is here. Stoicism used for what it was built for: staying steady while you run something hard.

Alfonso Graziano · AI

Learning AI-Native Software Engineering

A book on building software with AI as a working part of the process, from specifying the work to reviewing what comes back.

Why it is here. Where most of my thinking on handing work to an AI got sharpened. It shows up in Ten times zero.

Open the book

Robert C. Martin, 2008 · Code

Clean Code

A handbook of habits for writing code other people can read and change: naming, small functions, tests and refactoring.

Why it is here. Code is read far more often than it is written, so I write it for the next reader, who is usually me.

Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, 2020 edition · Process

The Scrum Guide

The official definition of Scrum, written by its two creators and kept short on purpose.

Why it is here. A guide rather than a book, so it stands thin too. The official version is short, and most of what teams call Scrum is not in it.

Read the guide

His seventy percent problem is the honest half of that. The machine gets you most of the way, and the last stretch is where unmanaged work rots. Every gate and handoff in my setup exists because of that last stretch.

    Brad Frostbradfrost.com

    Atomic design is more or less my love language for frontend work. Atoms compose into molecules, molecules into organisms, and nothing gets built big when it can be built from small parts. I would happily make fifty tiny components before one large one, and GRAIN, the design system under this site, is built in exactly that shape.
