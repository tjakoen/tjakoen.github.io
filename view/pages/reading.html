📚 Reading · the people I learn from

Most of what I build started as something I read.

These are the people whose work I keep going back to. Each entry says what I took from them and links to the place in my own writing where it shows up, so you can see the idea in use rather than take my word that it mattered. Go read them first. They said it better.

  • Atomic Design, Brad Frost, 2013. Free to read online, and the shape GRAIN is built in.
  • Beyond Vibe Coding, Addy Osmani. The seventy percent problem, and why the last stretch is where AI work rots.
  • Loop Engineering, Addy Osmani. An essay, not a book, so it stands thin on the shelf. It is the framing One loop, every repo applies.
  • The Personal MBA, Josh Kaufman, 2010. I learned business by starting five of them, and not one worked out the way I planned, which taught me more than success would have. This is the book I wish I had read before the first one.
  • Thinking, Fast and Slow, Daniel Kahneman, 2011. My first answer is usually the fast one, and the fast one is usually the one I should check.
  • The 48 Laws of Power, Robert Greene, 1998. I read it to recognise the moves, not to make them. Knowing how office politics work made it easier to stay out of them.
  • The Daily Stoic, Ryan Holiday and Stephen Hanselman, 2016. One page a morning, and most days it comes back to the same thing: what is mine to control, and what is not.
  • Aristotle's Way, Edith Hall, 2018. A good life is something you practise, the same way you get good at a craft.
  • How to Think Like a Roman Emperor, Donald Robertson, 2019. Stoicism used for what it was built for: staying steady while you run something hard.
  • Learning AI-Native Software Engineering, Alfonso Graziano. Where most of my thinking on handing work to an AI got sharpened.
  • Clean Code, Robert C. Martin, 2008. Code is read far more often than it is written, so I write it for the next reader, who is usually me.
  • The Scrum Guide, Ken Schwaber and Jeff Sutherland, 2020 edition. A guide rather than a book, so it stands thin too. The official version is short, and most of what teams call Scrum is not in it.

The people

  1. AO

    Addy Osmaniaddyosmani.com

    Addy gave me the words for what I was already fumbling toward with AI: durable work is a loop built from a few reusable parts, not one clever prompt. His seventy percent problem is the honest half of that. The machine gets you most of the way, and the last stretch is where unmanaged work rots. Every gate and handoff in my setup exists because of that last stretch.

    addyosmani.comLoop Engineering

    Shows up inOne loop, every repoTen times zero

  2. BF

    Brad Frostbradfrost.com

    Atomic design is more or less my love language for frontend work. Atoms compose into molecules, molecules into organisms, and nothing gets built big when it can be built from small parts. I would happily make fifty tiny components before one large one, and GRAIN, the design system under this site, is built in exactly that shape.

    atomicdesign.bradfrost.comAtomic Design

    Shows up inGRAINOrigin story

  3. C2

    Coding2GO@coding2go

    Coding2GO reminded me that the web platform grew up while I was not looking. Much of what I used to reach for a framework to do, the browser now does on its own, and that is a large part of why this site runs with no build step at all.

    youtube.comShort, practical videos on modern HTML, CSS and JavaScript

    Shows up inOrigin story

  4. AG

    Alfonso Grazianoalfonsograziano.it

    Alfonso's book is where most of my thinking on handing work to an AI got sharpened. Give a model one sentence of instruction and watch what it does first. If it guesses the stack or invents a folder, that gap is mine and not the model's, and the fix is to write down what I meant.

    alfonsograziano.itLearning AI-Native Software Engineering

    Shows up inTen times zero

  5. CM

    Cole Medin@coleam00

    Cole drew the clearest picture I have seen of what an AI-native project actually is, as one artifact rather than a list of parts. I borrowed his cross-section because it was better than the one I was carrying around in my head.

    github.comAI-native starter pack

    Shows up inBuild the floor
