🧭 Leading · the day job

Engineering manager at Career Team.

By day I lead a group of 15 to 25 engineers and product specialists at Career Team. A manager is judged on five things, and I find it more honest to show the work behind each one than to claim it. Pick one of the five below to see what it means and where I have written down how I do it, failures included.

Managing a team, in five parts

Leadership

What it means: deciding where the team goes, then getting out of its way. How I do it: I make the state of the work visible, so people can steer without waiting for me.

Show them the boardI shared my dashboard, and within two weeks the team had built their own.

Impact

What it means: the team's results, multiplied or divided by the manager. How I do it: I measure which of the two it was, and say so when the answer is divided.

The consoleone place for every review, ticket and run, built when the volume outgrew me.

Move bugs lefta direction from the top turned into a tool the whole team uses.

Operational excellence

What it means: the work runs the same way on a bad week as on a good one. How I do it: catch problems early, write the rules down, and let a machine enforce the ones it can.

The standardsthe written rules every repository and session follows.
One loop, every repothe same checks in every codebase, so nothing depends on who opened it.
Watch its handsreviewing what the work actually did, not what it reported.

Innovation

What it means: the team keeps pace with better tools instead of falling behind them. How I do it: I try the tool on my own work first, then hand the team something that already works. The largest piece of this is AI, which has its own section below.
