What status looks like without a hue

GRAIN is monochrome on purpose: --color-success and --color-danger both resolve to --ink. A button carries data-status, four accent tokens are repointed for it, and nothing consumes them, so the attribute renders as nothing. Connecting the tokens would render as nothing too. The question is not how to wire it. The question is what a status should look like when there is no hue to spend.

As it ships today

Three different intents, one rendering. This is the defect, live.

A. Fill

Destructive takes the solid ink fill and a paper label; everything else keeps the hairline. The loudest control on the page becomes the one that cannot be undone, which is either exactly right or exactly backwards depending on whether you think a Delete should attract the eye or avoid it.

B. Rule weight

Same ink, same fill, a doubled border on the destructive one. Quieter than A and it reads at a glance only once you know the vocabulary. It also survives a theme that changes the hairline colour, because it is a ratio rather than a value.

C. A mark

The destructive one carries a small filled block before its label, in the current colour. It reads in greyscale, in print and at any weight, and it is the only candidate that still works for someone who cannot see the difference between a 1px and a 2px rule. The cost is a new glyph vocabulary to keep consistent everywhere else.

D. Semantics only

Identical rendering, on purpose. data-status stops claiming a look and keeps its meaning for assistive tech and for the AI layer, while visual ranking stays data-variant's job, which is the axis that already works in a monochrome palette. This is the honest outcome if the answer is that a hueless status simply has nothing to say visually, and it is the only candidate that makes the code smaller.
