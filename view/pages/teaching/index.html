How the work is graded

Every course runs on a GitHub-native platform I built. Students submit real repositories; each activity is graded against a canonical test suite, and design work carries a written rubric. A badge is earned by completing its designated badging activities at the published 75% threshold, after instructor review and approval. Each award records the specific activities it recognizes. Badge pages explain the criteria, and individual certificates include verification details. Historical participation awards remain available while their eligibility is reconciled; they do not certify the new activity-based threshold.

AI in how I teach

AI runs through both sides of my teaching: what my students learn to do with it, and how I keep up with seven classes. The rule is the same on both sides. The AI drafts and a person decides.

I teach students to be worth multiplying

My position is that AI multiplies the engineer you already are, and ten times zero is still zero. I give that as a talk to student communities and hold to it in class: learn the fundamentals, then let the tool speed you up.

AI proposes the grade, I decide it

When my load went from four classes to seven, I built a course console where AI drafts a grade and its reasoning, and I approve or edit it before anything reaches a student. The time it saves goes back to feedback.
